Every day this researches a tech story, writes it up, draws two finished Instagram cards, and sends them to your phone with the caption ready to copy. You post them on @genphile.meme. Nothing reaches Instagram without you.

A normal day

  1. 1Research and writeAutomatic Six sources, around 160 candidates, scored for freshness, traction, topic and variety. The winner is summarised in plain words, then checked against its own source for invented numbers and copied sentences.
  2. 2Draw the cardsAutomatic Two 1080×1350 cards in your design, carrying your handle: the news story, and a programming concept told as a relationship. A card that would overflow its margins, or that Instagram would refuse, is thrown away rather than sent.
  3. 3Deliver to your phoneAutomatic Telegram sends each card as a file, so it is not squashed on the way, then the caption as a message of its own. Hold it, copy, done.
  4. 4You postYou Save the image, paste the caption, publish. About two minutes. Skip any card you don't like — nothing else happens, and the story never comes back.

Timing. GitHub's free scheduler drifts by hours, so the cards land somewhere between late morning and mid-afternoon IST rather than on a fixed minute. Post whenever suits you; 19:45 was only the habit the account was designed around.

If you ever want it to post by itself

The publisher is written, tested and parked. Switching it on takes two things, neither urgent:

MetaAn app, the account as a tester, and a token. Half an hour when their screens behave — which is exactly the part that didn't.
CloudflareA free account, then one command: powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File worker/deploy.ps1. About ten minutes, and no Meta involved.

Until both are done the publisher cannot run at all, and the repository variable LIVE stays unset as a second lock. Nothing you do by hand today gets in its way later.
