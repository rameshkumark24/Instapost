01 Three findings that reshape the plan

I checked the three assumptions your outline depends on before designing around them. Two of them don't hold as stated, and one adds a recurring chore nobody plans for. Everything after this section is designed around these answers.

Canva

Canva's Connect API can create designs, upload assets and export files on a free account — but the one endpoint you actually need, Autofill (push text and images into a saved template), requires both the integration developer and the acting user to belong to a Canva Enterprise organisation. There is a limited trial for paid plans during development, but nothing that survives into daily unattended production on a free account.

→ Canva stays as your design tool, not your runtime. You design the card in Canva once, then a free renderer reproduces it nightly. Section 05 covers the handoff.

Timing

GitHub Actions schedule events are explicitly best-effort. Free-plan private repositories sit at the bottom of the queue, and delays of 30–60 minutes are routinely reported; runs can be dropped entirely during peak load. A cron alone will not reliably hit 19:45.

→ Split the work: build early with slack, and fire the publish call from a Cloudflare Worker cron trigger, which is minute-accurate and free. Section 06.

Tokens

Instagram long-lived tokens last 60 days and die permanently if not refreshed inside that window. Most hobby automations silently break at day 61. There is a way out: a Meta Business system-user token can be issued with no expiry, which removes the treadmill entirely.

→ Prefer the system-user token. Keep a 30-day refresh job as belt-and-braces. Section 07.

What does hold

Publishing to your own account needs no Meta App Review. An app in Development mode, with your Instagram account added as a Tester, can publish indefinitely. This is the single biggest reason the project is genuinely free — and it is exactly the "private automation" case you described.

src/harvest.py

SOURCES = {
    "hn":     "https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?tags=story&numericFilters=points>120,created_at_i>{since}",
    "lobste": "https://lobste.rs/hottest.json",
    "devto":  "https://dev.to/api/articles?top=1&per_page=30",
    "gh":     "https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=pushed:>{date}+stars:>200&sort=stars",
    "arxiv":  "http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=cat:cs.AI&sortBy=submittedDate&max_results=25",
}

WEIGHTS = {"recency": 0.30, "engagement": 0.30, "fit": 0.25, "novelty": 0.15}

def score(item, ledger):
    if item.url in ledger or hits_blocklist(item.title):
        return 0.0                                   # hard reject
    hours = (now() - item.published).total_seconds() / 3600
    return (
        WEIGHTS["recency"]    * max(0, 1 - hours / 36)          # decays over 36h
        + WEIGHTS["engagement"] * log_norm(item.points, item.source)
        + WEIGHTS["fit"]        * keyword_fit(item.title, NICHE_TERMS)
        + WEIGHTS["novelty"]    * (1 - topic_overlap(item, ledger.last(7)))
    )

Picking a lane

"Tech" is too wide to build an audience on — the account reads as a random aggregator and the scorer has nothing to optimise against. Pick one lane and set NICHE_TERMS to match. Reasonable options: AI/ML shipping news, developer tooling, security incidents, open-source releases, or systems/infrastructure. You can widen later; you cannot easily un-blur a fuzzy account.

Composition, and the honesty constraint

The LLM never states facts. It receives the title, the source's own summary and the URL, and its only jobs are to compress into a headline of ≤ 9 words and write a hook. Any claim not present in the input is a bug. This matters legally as well as editorially — it is the difference between an original summary and a fabrication published under your name.

caption template

{hook_line}                        <- keyword-rich, this is what search indexes

{two_sentence_summary}

Source: {publication} — link in bio / see comments
{5_to_8_hashtags}

Hashtags are not the lever they used to be

Instagram removed hashtag-following and has repeatedly said hashtags do little for reach; caption keywords now drive in-app search. Use 5–8 genuinely relevant tags for classification and put your real effort into the first line. Piling on 30 tags reads as spam signal, not distribution.

05 Stage 4 — the Canva substitution

Your outline says "load into Canva". As established in section 01, the API that would do that is Enterprise-gated. Here are the three real options, honestly ranked.

OptionAutomatableCostVerdict
Canva Enterprise + Autofill API Fully Enterprise contract Exactly what you described, and the only thing that breaks your zero-cash rule. Requires a sales conversation, not a signup form.
Canva free + manual export No ₹0 You'd hand-make a card every evening. That's the thing you're automating away.
Canva design → HTML/CSS clone Fully ₹0 Recommended. Same visual result, no tier gate, no API quota, no vendor dependency in the hot path.

The handoff, concretely

  1. Design the card once in Canva's free editor at 1080 × 1350 (4:5 — the tallest ratio Instagram accepts, so it occupies the most feed real estate).
  2. Export the static layers — background, texture, logo lockup — as a single PNG. Delete the text layers from that export.
  3. Note the exact geometry of each text block: x, y, width, size, weight, line-height, colour.
  4. Rebuild those text blocks as absolutely-positioned HTML over the PNG background. This is 60–80 lines of CSS, written once.
  5. Playwright screenshots the page at a 1080×1350 viewport. Output is byte-for-byte reproducible.

You keep Canva for what it's genuinely best at — designing the thing — and drop it from the part that has to run unattended at 18:33 every night.

Font licensing — the trap almost everyone hits

Fonts licensed to you through Canva are licensed for use inside Canva. Downloading the font file and embedding it in your own renderer is outside that grant. Build the template on an SIL Open Font License or Apache-licensed family from the start — the Google Fonts catalogue is almost entirely safe — and this problem never exists.

src/render.py

from playwright.sync_api import sync_playwright

W, H = 1080, 1350                       # 4:5, the tallest feed ratio IG accepts

def render(post, out="dist/card.jpg"):
    html = TEMPLATE.render(
        headline=post["headline"],      # <= 9 words, auto-fit via CSS clamp()
        body=post["body"],
        source=post["publication"],
        date=post["date"],
    )
    with sync_playwright() as p:
        page = p.chromium.launch().new_page(viewport={"width": W, "height": H})
        page.set_content(html, wait_until="networkidle")
        page.wait_for_timeout(300)      # let webfonts settle before the shot
        page.screenshot(path=out, type="jpeg", quality=90)
    return out

Encode JPEG, not PNG

The Instagram content-publishing endpoint documents JPEG for image containers. PNG uploads are a common source of unexplained container failures. Render straight to JPEG at quality 90 and stay under the 8 MB ceiling — you'll land around 300 KB.

06 Stage 5–7 — hosting, timing, publishing

Why the image needs a public URL

You cannot POST image bytes to the container endpoint. You give Meta an image_url and their servers fetch it. That single fact dictates the hosting design: the file must be publicly readable at publish time, from Meta's network, with no auth.

A public GitHub repo gives you that for free at raw.githubusercontent.com. The pipeline commits the JPEG at 18:34 and the Worker references it at 19:45 — 70 minutes is ample for CDN propagation, and stage 5 verifies a 200 before it proceeds anyway.

Public repo is a deliberate choice, not a compromise

It buys unmetered Actions minutes, better scheduler priority, and free image hosting. Credentials live in encrypted GitHub Secrets and are never readable from a public repo. What is public is your source code and your content history — for a personal tech account, that's a non-issue and arguably a small portfolio asset.

Hitting 19:45 on the nose

The Actions cron fires at 13:00 UTC with 45 minutes of slack — if it's 40 minutes late, nothing downstream notices. The Worker cron fires at 15 14 * * * and does the two API calls that make the post public. Workers cron has one-minute granularity and no queue behind it.

worker/src/index.js

export default {
  async scheduled(event, env, ctx) {
    const base = `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/${env.REPO}/main/dist`;
    const post = await (await fetch(`${base}/post.json?t=${Date.now()}`)).json();

    if (post.hold) return;                          // abort button was tapped
    if (post.date !== todayIST()) {                 // build never ran — don't repost yesterday
      return alert(env, "No fresh post for today; build job likely failed.");
    }

    const G = `https://graph.facebook.com/vXX.0/${env.IG_USER_ID}`;

    const c = await post_(`${G}/media`, {
      image_url: `${base}/card.jpg`,
      caption:   post.caption,
      access_token: env.IG_TOKEN,
    });
    if (c.error) return alert(env, `container failed: ${c.error.message}`);

    const r = await post_(`${G}/media_publish`, {
      creation_id: c.id,
      access_token: env.IG_TOKEN,
    });
    if (r.error) return alert(env, `publish failed: ${r.error.message}`);

    await alert(env, `Live: https://www.instagram.com/p/${r.id}`);
  },
};

09 Legal

The one that can actually cost you the account

Automating Instagram through the official Graph API is permitted and is what this design does. Automating it by driving the app or website — instagrapi, instabot, Selenium against instagram.com, or any "no API key needed" library — breaches the Platform Terms and is actively detected. The penalty is account restriction or permanent loss. There is no version of this project worth that shortcut.

Platform terms

  • Publishing automation is fine. Engagement automation is not — no auto-follow, auto-like, auto-DM or auto-comment. Keep this system write-only to your own feed.
  • Dev mode is a legitimate end state for a single-user app. You are not evading review; review exists for apps acting on other people's accounts.
  • Respect the publishing quota (25–50 posts per rolling 24 h depending on the doc you read). At one post a day you have three orders of magnitude of headroom.

Copyright

  • Facts aren't protected; expression is. "Company X shipped Y today" is free to state. The article's sentences describing it are not free to copy.
  • Write your own summary, every time. This is the whole legal basis of the account. Never paste source paragraphs into the card or caption, and never let the LLM reproduce them verbatim.
  • Attribute by name. Not legally sufficient on its own, but it establishes good faith, and it's what turns "scraper" into "curator" in a human reader's eyes.
  • Never use the publisher's images. Press photos, article hero images and paywalled screenshots are the highest-risk asset you could put on a card. Your generated card sidesteps this entirely — a real advantage of the design in section 05.
  • India (your likely jurisdiction): §52 of the Copyright Act 1957 provides fair dealing for reporting current events and for criticism or review. Note it is narrower than US fair use — it protects enumerated purposes, not a general balancing test. Original summary plus attribution sits comfortably inside it.
  • US readers: 17 U.S.C. §107 fair use; a short transformative summary with attribution is a strong posture.

Trademarks and fonts

  • Naming a company to refer to that company is nominative use and is fine. Making its logo your card's dominant visual, or implying endorsement, is not.
  • Icon sets like Simple Icons are CC0 — but CC0 covers the SVG file, never the trademark it depicts. Public-domain artwork does not grant brand rights.
  • Font licensing is covered in section 05. It is the most commonly breached licence in exactly this kind of project.

AI disclosure

  • Meta requires labelling of realistic AI-generated media and applies automatic labels where it detects them. Text cards on a designed background are not realistic synthetic media and don't trigger this.
  • If you ever add AI-generated photographic imagery, label it. The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations for synthetic content became applicable in August 2026 — worth knowing about now rather than after the fact, even though a personal account has limited exposure.
  • An LLM writing your caption is not itself a disclosable event under any current rule. Many creators disclose anyway; that's an editorial call, not a legal one.

Data protection and disclosure

  • You process no third-party personal data — only public article metadata and your own credentials. India's DPDP Act 2023 and the GDPR are effectively out of scope.
  • Credentials in GitHub Secrets and Worker secrets, never in the repo. A leaked long-lived token is a full account-publishing compromise.
  • No sponsorship means no disclosure duty. The moment anyone pays you for a post, ASCI guidelines (India) or FTC endorsement rules (US) require clear labelling in the post itself.

This is an engineering read of publicly documented terms and statutes, not legal advice. For a personal account posting original summaries with attribution, the risk profile is genuinely low — but the analysis above is where to start if that ever changes.
